# Handover — pack distribution, odds, and what the mint has to implement

For Breno. Written 2026-08-19, revised the same day after the tiers moved to scoring.
Supersedes §4.1 option 3 of [`breno-mint-and-infra.md`](breno-mint-and-infra.md), which is
otherwise still current.

Two things changed since that document. The rarity curve was rebuilt
([`../rarity-and-booster-plan.md`](../rarity-and-booster-plan.md)), and we proved that the
way the shop deals packs today cannot survive a real supply cap
([`../distribution-proof.md`](../distribution-proof.md)).

The short version of both: **the mint sells sealed packs, and the pull resolves against a
Bitcoin block that has not been mined at the time of sale.** That is the whole design. The
rest of this document is the numbers you need and the algorithm that produces them.

Nothing here is implemented. `scripts/build_shop_data.py` still emits the old flat box.

## 1 · The set

285 collectible cards in five tiers, plus 10 Basic Resources that are uncapped, free, and
never pulled. Card ids are `E1-001` … `E1-295` and are stable.

**Tiers are not inherited from the 1993 reference.** Every card is scored on three axes —
market value, real playability, and reference rarity, weighted 0.40 / 0.40 / 0.20 — and the
tiers are rank cuts on that score. A card that was common in 1993 but is expensive and
played today ends up scarce here. Scores are in `cards/e1-card-scores.json`; the full table
with every card, its rank and its axes is `docs/e1-tier-census.md`.

| tier | cards | copies each | total copies | share of mint | score band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Genesis | 9 | 21 | 189 | 0.15% | 0.715–0.820 |
| Vault | 21 | 63 | 1,323 | 1.05% | 0.582–0.714 |
| Rare | 90 | 216 | 19,440 | 15.48% | 0.410–0.581 |
| Uncommon | 75 | 279 | 20,925 | 16.66% | 0.263–0.406 |
| Common | 90 | 930 | 83,700 | 66.65% | 0.117–0.261 |
| **total** | **285** | | **125,577** | **100%** | |

One mint = **20,925 packs**. Pack = **7 cards: 4 Common + 1 Uncommon + 1 Prime**, plus
**1 Basic Resource, guaranteed, uncapped and free**. Six paid cards, seven in the wrapper.

The Prime slot draws from a single pool of 20,952 holding all Rare, Vault and Genesis. It
is the only slot where tier is uncertain.

There are more Rares than Uncommons on purpose. With one slot per pack, the chance of a
*named* card is driven by how many cards share that slot, so a fat Rare tier is what makes
any single Rare hard to find.

Machine-readable census: `cards/e1-tier-census.json`, regenerated by
`scripts/build_tier_census.py`. That file is the source of truth for every number below.

## 3 · Why the mint cannot just roll dice per pack

Full proof in [`../distribution-proof.md`](../distribution-proof.md). The operational
summary:

If each pack is an independent draw with fixed odds, supply is a random variable, not a
cap. Over 20,925 packs a 21-copy card lands anywhere in **[12, 30]** at 95% confidence.
Across the nine Genesis cards there is a **12.8%** chance at least one exceeds 1.5× its cap
and a **5.5%** chance at least one ends below half supply. Relative error scales as
$1/\sqrt{T}$, so the model is least accurate exactly where accuracy is the product.

A cap therefore requires drawing without replacement. That is not the same as pre-shuffling
the box: the urn only fixes *what is in it*, not *what order it comes out in*. The order can
be — and should be — decided later, by a beacon nobody controls.

**What the mint must not do:** publish a pre-shuffled order. The current
`site/shop-data.js` ships `seed: 600` plus the whole ordered array in plaintext, which
means every pack is mapped before the first sale. Do not reproduce that.

## 4 · The mechanism

### 4.1 Shape

1. **Commit to the census, not the order.** Before selling, publish
   `commitment = SHA-256(canonical_counts_json ‖ 0x00 ‖ salt ‖ 0x00)`. Counts only. There
   is no order yet, so there is nothing to leak.
2. **Sell sealed packs.** A purchase binds the pack to a future Bitcoin block height
   `H = h_pay + 1`. The buyer receives a pack token naming `pack_id` and `H`.
3. **Open against the beacon.** Once block `H` exists and has 2 further confirmations, the
   pack resolves deterministically from that block hash. Neither buyer nor mint knows the
   contents before `H` is mined.
4. **Chain the log.** After each pack, publish the new state hash and the updated remaining
   counts. Anyone can replay the whole mint from the census, the public block hashes and
   the log.

### 4.2 The draw

Canonical order is ascending card id within each pool. `h(...)` is SHA-256 over each part
followed by a `0x00` separator.

```python
def draw(counts, order, rho, slot):
    """Pick one card from the remaining pool. counts: {id: n}, order: canonical id list."""
    R = sum(counts[c] for c in order)
    x = int.from_bytes(h(rho, slot), "big") % R
    for cid in order:
        if x < counts[cid]:
            counts[cid] -= 1
            return cid
        x -= counts[cid]
    raise AssertionError("pool exhausted")

def open_pack(census, pools, beacon, pack_id):
    rho = h(bytes.fromhex(beacon), pack_id)
    out, slot = [], 0
    for pool_name, n_slots in (("common", 4), ("uncommon", 1), ("prime", 1)):
        for _ in range(n_slots):
            out.append(draw(census, pools[pool_name], rho, slot))
            slot += 1
    return out
```

Modulo bias is bounded by `R / 2^256` and is negligible at `R ≤ 62,974`.

State chain, one entry per pack:

```
state_0 = commitment
state_k = h(state_{k-1}, pack_id, beacon_hash, ",".join(cards))
```

### 4.4 Decisions baked into the above

- **Duplicates within a pack are allowed.** 6.52% of packs hold two of the same common.
  Rejecting them would need a redraw loop, which complicates replay for a cosmetic gain. If
  you want them rejected, say so now, not after the census is committed.
- **Basic Resources are not in any pool.** They are uncapped and handed out free by the
  client. Scarcity must never block deck building.
- **Reorgs.** Bind at `H = h_pay + 1`, resolve only at `H + 2`. If `H` is reorged before
  resolution the pack simply re-binds to the new block at that height. After resolution the
  log entry is final; do not rewrite it.

### 4.5 Beacon security

A miner is the only party who can influence the beacon, and only by discarding a block they
found.

| | |
|---|---|
| whole mint revenue at 126 sat/pack | **0.0264 BTC** |
| discarding one block to re-roll | **3.125 BTC** + fees |
| one re-grind as a multiple of the entire mint | **119×** |
| chance a re-grind hits a chosen Genesis | 0.001 |
| expected cost to force one Genesis | **3,119 BTC** ≈ 118,000× the mint |

If that margin ever narrows, fold in a buyer-supplied nonce via commit–reveal; the result is
unbiasable if *either* party is honest.

## 5 · HTTP contract we would like

Same rules as the last handover: browser-callable (CORS), NIP-98 auth over a NIP-07 signer,
no accounts, and every failure must land on a definite sentence rather than a spinner.

| method | path | purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `GET` | `/mint/census` | committed counts, salt commitment, pack price, pack size, tier map |
| `GET` | `/mint/state` | current `state_k`, packs sold, remaining counts per card |
| `GET` | `/mint/log?from=&to=` | replayable draw log: `pack_id`, `height`, `beacon`, `cards`, `state` |
| `POST` | `/pack` | buy `count` sealed packs → `[{pack_id, height, invoice}]` |
| `POST` | `/pack/{pack_id}/open` | resolve once `height + 2` is reached → `{cards, beacon, state}` |
| `GET` | `/collection/{pubkey}` | cards attributed to a pubkey |

`POST /pack?count=<n>` is what `site/shop.js` assumes today and we are not attached to it —
tell us the real shape and we will follow it.

Two things we need in the response bodies regardless of shape:

- **`height` before payment settles**, so the UI can say "this pack resolves against block
  N, which does not exist yet" — that sentence is the entire fairness argument and the
  player should read it before paying.
- **`beacon` and `state` on open**, so the client can verify the draw itself rather than
  trusting the answer.

## 6 · Two parameters that need your call

### 6.1 Price, and who can buy the whole thing

At the current `PRICE_MSAT = 21000` per card, a pack is **126 sat** and the entire mint is
**0.0264 BTC**. One buyer with pocket change can drain it, at which point the scarcity is
real but meaningless — 21 Genesis Lotus exist and one person has all 21.

| per-pubkey cap | distinct pubkeys needed to drain the mint |
|---|---|
| 10 packs | 2,092 |
| 50 packs | 418 |
| 100 packs | 209 |
| 500 packs | 42 |

A pubkey is free to create, so a cap is a speed bump, not a defence. The honest levers are
price, a per-pubkey cap, and time-gated release tranches. We have no strong preference and
this is squarely mint-side policy — but the odds tables above are worthless as a marketing
claim if the answer is "one wallet took the lot in an afternoon".

### 6.2 The endgame

A hard cap creates a computable edge late in the mint, in any scheme, physical cardboard
included. If no Genesis has surfaced by 90% depletion, all 189 sit in the last 2,092 packs:
9.03% per pack against a 0.90% base rate, a **10×** edge that anyone reading
`/mint/state` can compute.

Three responses, in our order of preference: retire the final tranche and never sell it;
publish the chained commitment live but reveal the full log only at close; or state the
effect plainly and let people play the tail on purpose. We lean toward the first. It is
your inventory, so it is your call.

## 7 · Constraints, unchanged

§4.3 of the previous handover still holds in full. Restating the two that bite hardest
here:

1. **The game client never custodies funds.** The mint may custody — that is what a mint
   is — but the boundary must be visible and stated on the page.
2. **Nothing may block play.** Every one of the 295 cards is playable by anyone, for free,
   with no mint and no network. Ownership is a collectible layer on top. A mint outage must
   never stop a match, and deck building is not ownership-gated unless we take that product
   decision explicitly, which we have not.

Adding one for this design:

3. **The order must not exist before the sale.** No pre-shuffle, no published seed, no
   ordered box. If the mint can predict a pack, so eventually can everyone else.
