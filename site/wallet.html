Send a card

The mint spends your proof and signs a replacement locked to the key you name. It happens once and it cannot be reversed, so the card is named back to you before anything is sent.

This is the P2BK destination from their own wallet page — not an npub, not a Lightning address. The mint locks the new proof to it, so a typo sends the card to a key nobody holds.

Sending spends the card at the mint. There is no take-back and no undo.

Only the key you named can claim these, so any channel will do. But each is the only copy of its transfer and they live on this page alone — copy them out before you close the tab, because nothing here can produce them a second time.

How a transfer works

A card is a card and not a copy because the mint will only ever spend a proof once. A transfer asks it to do exactly that: burn the proof this wallet holds and sign a new one locked to the key you named.

Which is why sending is final. The moment the mint answers, the card belongs to them — there is no recall, no expiry and nobody to appeal to. Read the name and the key on the confirmation, not on the form.

If the answer never arrives — the tab closes, the network drops — the card is not lost. The outputs that claim the replacement stay in this wallet as a pending transfer, and Finish it now asks the mint for the same answer again under the same idempotency key. Do that before assuming anything is gone.

A token handed to you is locked to your destination key: nobody else can claim it, and importing is what moves it in. Check it first — the mint has the last word on whether it is real, but you should know what you are accepting before you accept it.
